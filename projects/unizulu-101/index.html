University of Zululand · 2016–2021

UNIZULU 101

A cross-faculty core curriculum pilot, built for institutional integration and adapted for emergency remote learning.

← Explore all projects
The intervention

Institutional need

The pilot needed to operate across faculties, programmes and campuses, with a coordinated delivery support structure. The COVID-19 disruption then required a rapid change in delivery.

My ownership

Designed the first-year core curriculum pilot; supervised 40+ postgraduate student assistants; served as programme liaison to faculty managers and institutional stakeholders, with standing invitations to faculty and teaching and learning boards. Developed the facilitator guide that prepared the assistants to run small-group dialogue sessions and support students through a five-stage major assignment.

From design to delivery

Implementation

Integrated the module into academic structures across four faculties, ten programmes and two campuses. Designed and led the emergency remote learning transition, adapting curriculum delivery to the new context. The online design was built on five strategies: objective-based learning bits, personalised assessment and content, adaptive learning paths, alternative content formats and procedurally generated questions, all read through a humanising pedagogy lens.

Delivery evidence

Maintained 75%+ engagement among 1,200+ students during the remote transition. This is an engagement measure, rather than a claim of independently measured learning or workplace performance gains.

A 2021 end-of-module evaluation drew 955 student responses: 73% agreed the module was good for first-year students, 71% that its learning objectives were clear and 65% that it helped them adjust to university life; 11% said they did not understand the module. Moodle navigation scored lowest, at 60% agreement, which made the online experience the clearest area to improve.
