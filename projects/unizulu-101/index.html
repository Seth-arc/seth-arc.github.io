UNIZULU 101

Delivered · cross-faculty pilot

A first-year core curriculum pilot across four faculties and two campuses, carried into small groups by more than 40 postgraduate assistants and redesigned for remote delivery when COVID-19 closed the campuses.

The design problem

The challenge

The pilot had to operate across faculties, programmes and campuses, with a coordinated support structure. The COVID-19 disruption then required a rapid change in how it was delivered.

The logic

One curriculum, many classrooms. Build the module into existing academic structures, carry it into small-group dialogue through trained postgraduate assistants, and when teaching moved online, redesign it around five strategies read through a humanising pedagogy lens.

What students produce

A five-stage major assignment, supported by the assistants in small-group sessions and submitted through Moodle.

My role

I designed the first-year core curriculum pilot, supervised the 40+ postgraduate student assistants, and served as programme liaison to faculty managers and institutional stakeholders, with standing invitations to faculty and teaching and learning boards. I developed the facilitator guide that prepared the assistants to run small-group dialogue sessions and support students through the major assignment.

From design to delivery

Integration

The module was built into academic structures across four faculties, ten programmes and two campuses.

The remote transition

I designed and led the emergency remote learning transition, adapting curriculum delivery to the new context. The online design rested on five strategies: objective-based learning bits, personalised assessment and content, adaptive learning paths, alternative content formats and procedurally generated questions.

The assistants

Graduate Academic Assistants ran small-group dialogue sessions using a facilitator guide written for the 2020 pilot.

Design decisions

Evidence

What was measured

Engagement held above 75% among 1,200+ students during the remote transition. A 2021 end-of-module evaluation drew 955 student responses: 73% agreed the module was good for first-year students, 71% that its learning objectives were clear and 65% that it helped them adjust to university life; 11% said they did not understand the module.

Limits

What the evidence can’t show

The 75% figure is an engagement measure, not independently measured learning or later performance, and the evaluation is students’ own view. Moodle navigation scored lowest, at 60% agreement, which made the online experience the clearest area to improve.
