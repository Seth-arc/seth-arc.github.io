Independent learning demonstration

Starlink Kit Setup

Portfolio demonstration

A 15–20 minute computer-based training module for new Tier-1 support agents. It moves from how the signal chain works to a live troubleshooting conversation, with practice and feedback at every step.

← Explore all projects
Learner
New Tier-1 support agents learning residential kit installation
Length
15–20 minutes across five lessons
Design frame
Merrill’s First Principles of Instruction, situated cognition, scaffolded practice and formative feedback
Built with
HTML, CSS, vanilla JavaScript and Three.js; designed to be SCORM- and xAPI-ready
The design problem

The challenge

A support team that is scaling fast needs agents who can talk customers through installation problems without a physical kit to train on, and without support quality slipping.

The logic

Task-centred, scaffolded practice. Agents build a model of the system, see it in 3D, put the setup in order, then handle a customer conversation, with immediate corrective feedback on every choice, including the tempting wrong ones.

What learners produce

A correct setup sequence, diagnostic choices in a live scenario and knowledge-check answers, with experience points tracking progress.

My role

I designed and built the module as an independent portfolio piece.

Inside the module

A demonstration, stated as one

This is an independent demonstration, not affiliated with Starlink or SpaceX. No employment, client relationship or measured performance gains are claimed. The design targets were to halve time-to-proficiency from ten days to five and to cut escalations by 30%; these are targets, not results.

Five lessons

Explore, see, sequence, decide, check.

Each lesson uses a different kind of practice.

01

How it works

Explore and discover: click through the signal chain from satellite to dish to router to device, and see why knowing where a fault sits matters.

02

Constellation 3D

Rotate and zoom a simulated 240-satellite constellation, with hotspots on orbital shells, coverage, scale and latency.

03

Kit setup sequence

Sequencing and recall: drag the installation steps into the right order.

04

Customer troubleshooting

Scenario-based decisions: choose a response at each turn of a customer conversation and get feedback on every option.

05

Knowledge check

Three questions to confirm the essentials before the agent moves on.

Design decisions

Every lesson has a job.

The module trains agents to diagnose before they act.

DecisionWhat it trainsIn the module
Start from the signal chain A mental model to diagnose from. Agents explore each link in the chain and why knowing where a problem sits leads to the right questions.
Make the invisible visible Understanding orbit, coverage and latency. A 3D globe of 240 simulated satellites, with orbits that can be shown and four hotspots to explore.
Sequence before troubleshooting Recalling the procedure customers will ask about. Drag-to-order setup steps.
Practise the conversation, not just the facts Applying knowledge where it’s actually used. A customer reports intermittent drops, and the agent picks each response.
Feedback on every option Learning why a tempting answer is wrong. Escalating straight to Tier 2 is flagged: it lengthens the customer’s wait and overloads the next tier.
Diagnose before replacing Resisting the costly default. An obstruction alert points to placement, not hardware, so a replacement order is flagged as wasted effort.
Evidence

What’s documented

The module is complete and playable in the browser as a portfolio demonstration.

Limits

What the evidence can’t show

It hasn’t been used with real agents or an employer, so there are no learner results, and the time-to-proficiency and escalation targets are untested.
