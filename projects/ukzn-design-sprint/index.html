UKZN Design Sprint

Connecting institutional digital learning strategy with faculty design practice.

← Explore all projects
The intervention

Institutional need

Digital learning development required shared design standards and practical faculty support, informed by root-cause analysis with academic leadership. College surveys, problem-tree and objective-tree workshops, benchmarking against four universities and a digital literacy survey with 1,633 respondents set the priorities: mobile-first, bilingual and differentiated learning.

My ownership

Led a team of four instructional designers, established design standards, supported continuous professional development and worked with university leadership on digital learning strategy. Designed the Module Design Sprints programme and the E-Learning for Lecturers course.

From design to delivery

Implementation

A five-part faculty development workshop series drew on ADDIE, SAM and multimedia learning principles, giving faculty practical cycles for developing modules. The Module Design Sprints ran as a 10-week online programme of five two-hour sprints, each ending in a working deliverable: a learning outcomes framework, a module storyboard and lesson plan, an assessment plan and rubric, an accessible module layout, and an improvement action plan. E-Learning for Lecturers was a five-week, self-paced Moodle course with in-video questions, peer review of each other’s activities and an evaluation prompt after every unit. Worked with Quality Assurance on NQF-compliant curriculum and digital learning practices.

Delivery evidence

14 workshops with 297 attendees, 21 module redesigns across four colleges, and the university’s inaugural Instructional Design Expo. Blended Teaching, Student Engagement and EdTech toolkits supported academic staff, and a Blended Module Style Guide set accessibility rules for alt text, captions and transcripts, and isiZulu translation of instructions where possible.
